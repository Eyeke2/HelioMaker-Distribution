PHD2 Calibration and Troubleshooting

Proper calibration in PHD2 is essential to ensure accurate guiding and optimal performance during imaging sessions. Before starting the calibration process, make sure to configure HelioMaker detection and PHD2 guiding settings. It is highly recommended to verify that object detection is stable over a few guiding samples before initiating calibration. When calibrating on the Moon, it is also strongly recommended to enable Dynamic Target Tracking first, as the correct dynamic lunar rate can substantially reduce drift and improve calibration quality. If the mount is properly polar aligned, this can result in practically no orthogonality error during PHD2 calibration on the Moon. Dynamic tracking may also be useful for solar imaging, although the improvement is usually smaller than for the Moon. For detailed information and examples for setting detection parameters please refer to Find & Track. The same detector interface is used with a shared camera and with a supported physical PHD2 camera. For standalone and fallback physical-camera behavior, see Find & Track with a Physical PHD2 Camera.

Preparing for Calibration

Several factors influence the success and accuracy of PHD2 calibration:

  • Polar Alignment: Ensure your mount is accurately polar-aligned to minimize drift and improve guiding efficiency.

    For detailed steps on how to align your mount during daytime imaging, refer to the Daytime Polar Alignment Using PHD2 chapter.

    For lunar calibration in particular, combining good polar alignment with Dynamic Target Tracking can significantly improve calibration stability and may result in practically no orthogonality error.

  • Mount Mechanical Condition: Proper maintenance of your mount, such as minimizing backlash, can significantly enhance calibration accuracy and overall guiding performance.

Using Calibration Tools

PHD2 provides several tools to assist with calibration and guiding optimization:

  • Calibration Assistant: While this tool helps evaluate calibration settings and provides recommendations, it is limited for daytime use because calibration must be performed "here." For more details, see the PHD2 Tools documentation.

  • HelioMaker Calibration: When performing calibration via HelioMaker, either using the Calibrate button or as part of a sequence, HelioMaker automatically computes the optimum calibration step size and number of steps. The PHD2 Calibration Pre-flight Dialog is shown before calibration starts, allowing HelioMaker to use accurate solar or lunar ephemeris declination when calibrating on the Sun or Moon. If you choose to calibrate using PHD2's internal tools, you will need to set the calibration parameters manually.

Post-Calibration Optimization

After completing calibration, you can optionally use PHD2's Guiding Assistant (GA) to measure guiding performance and suggest improved guiding settings, such as minimum and maximum motion thresholds. Note the following:

  • Exposure Time: When accepting GA's suggestions for exposure time, apply the recommended value to the Exposure + Time Lapse setting rather than just the exposure. The exposure setting itself should be determined based on image quality considerations for solar, lunar, and planetary imaging.

  • Time Lapse: Adjust the Time Lapse parameter in PHD2's Planetary and Solar Guiding Tool as needed to achieve the desired guiding frequency (or sampling period). Note that HelioMaker enforces a minimum guiding interval (the sum of exposure and time lapse values in milliseconds), which can be configured in its PHD2 Configuration settings (accessible via the PHD2 gear button).

Understanding the PHD2 Calibration Graph

The PHD2 calibration graph shows how the guide target moved while PHD2 sent calibration pulses to the mount. In HelioMaker it is shown in the Session area after valid calibration data is available; until then the plot area may appear empty or dark.

The RA and Dec lines show how the mount axes map onto the camera sensor's X/Y directions. These lines should be roughly perpendicular, but their overall angle on the screen is not important. The calibration points will rarely be perfectly spaced or perfectly aligned. What matters is that the paths are reasonably straight and do not show major curves, sharp turns, or reversals.

Solid points represent the west and north calibration moves used to calculate the RA and Dec guide rates. Hollow points show the return paths for the east and south moves. These return paths are useful for spotting seeing-related fluctuation and mechanical backlash. If the return path looks delayed, offset, or irregular, the mount may have backlash or inconsistent response. If PHD2 is configured to use fast recentering, the return paths may contain fewer points.

Good polar alignment reduces drift during calibration and helps the RA and Dec axes appear clean and orthogonal. A mechanically healthy, well-balanced mount with secure connections and minimal backlash will usually produce a cleaner calibration graph and more reliable guiding corrections.

Calibration quality also depends on an accurate focal length setting in HelioMaker. When the focal length is set correctly, HelioMaker can better estimate the calibration step size and the expected number of calibration points PHD2 should use. An incorrect focal length can make the calibration moves too small or too large, causing PHD2 to complete calibration with too many or too few steps, which can make the resulting calibration graph less reliable.

Troubleshooting Calibration Issues

If calibration fails or guiding performance is suboptimal, consider the following:

  • Ensure proper mount leveling, polar alignment, and verify that the target is detectable with stable and consistent settings in HelioMaker's Find & Track tool. Accurate mount leveling helps minimize tracking errors, while polar alignment ensures the mount's axis is aligned with the Earth's rotational axis, reducing drift over time. Before calibration, confirm that the target is clearly visible and consistently detected using the appropriate guiding mode, such as Full Disk/Crescent or Surface Features. Adjust camera exposure and gain at the camera source and tune detection sensitivity in Find & Track. If HelioMaker reported that the physical-camera detection link is unavailable, use PHD2's local detector controls for that fallback session.

  • Inspect the mount for mechanical issues, such as excessive backlash, and address them before recalibrating. Backlash occurs when there is a delay in the mount's response to guiding corrections, often caused by looseness or gaps in the gears or drivetrain. Excessive backlash can lead to inaccurate guiding and calibration results. Check the mount's gear tension and adjust it according to the manufacturer's recommendations. Lubricate moving parts if necessary and ensure all connections, including the dovetail saddle and counterweights, are secure. Additionally, verify that the mount is properly balanced in both RA and Dec axes to reduce strain on the motors and improve overall performance.

  • Avoid Calibration near the mount flip window: Do not start calibration so close to the meridian that a pier flip could be triggered mid-calibration, leave a safety buffer (for example 20-40 minutes, roughly 5-10° east or west).

  • In case guiding stops working after a meridian flip and the mount drives the target out of its place, consider toggling the PHD2's Reverse Dec output after meridian flip checkbox in its Advanced Settings (accessible via the "brain" button). This setting tells PHD2 how to adjust its guiding corrections based on the mount's behavior after a meridian flip. Some mounts automatically reverse their declination motor behavior, while others require PHD2 to handle the reversal. Experiment with this setting to determine the correct configuration for your mount.

  • Review PHD2's calibration graph and adjust calibration step size or guiding parameters as needed. The calibration graph provides a visual representation of how the mount responds to guiding commands along the RA and Dec axes. Look for a clean, orthogonal pattern with evenly spaced calibration steps. If the steps are too short or too long, it may indicate that the calibration step size is not optimized for your setup. In such cases:
    • Step Size: Adjust the calibration step size in PHD2's Advanced Settings. For long focal lengths or slow mounts, increase the step size to ensure the mount moves a measurable distance during calibration. For shorter focal lengths or faster mounts, decrease the step size to avoid excessive movement.
    • Orthogonality: Ensure that the RA and Dec axes are nearly perpendicular in the calibration graph. Significant deviations from 90 degrees may indicate polar alignment issues or mechanical problems, such as differential flexure or mount imbalance, which should be addressed before recalibrating.
    • Number of Steps: Aim for at least 12 well-defined steps in each direction. If the number of steps is too few, consider increasing the calibration step size or the exposure duration to allow sufficient movement during each step.
    After making these adjustments, repeat the calibration process and verify the updated graph for improvements. A well-tuned calibration ensures that guiding corrections are applied effectively, minimizing drift and optimizing tracking accuracy during your imaging session.
