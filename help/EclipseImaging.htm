Solar Eclipse Imaging

HelioMaker includes a dedicated workflow for planning, rehearsing, and running an automated capture of a total solar eclipse. It coordinates the selected eclipse plan with SharpCap Pro camera control, optional PHD2 guiding, precise contact timing, operator cues, preflight checks, and a live run console.

This chapter is an introduction to the feature. The separate Solar Eclipse Imaging Guide provides detailed instructions organized by task and should be read well before eclipse day.

What Makes HelioMaker Different

HelioMaker brings the entire solar eclipse imaging workflow into one application. It finds the eclipse for your site, helps you design an exposure plan across every phase, and then runs that plan automatically. It drives the camera through SharpCap Pro, guides on the Sun through the custom PHD2, manages the HDR exposure brackets, and can optionally refocus a few minutes before the central phase. Total, annular and partial eclipses are each planned in their own right, with pacing, cues and safety wording that follow the eclipse you are actually observing.

It uses a detailed physical model with precise ephemerides for eclipse phases and contact timing, optional atmospheric refraction correction, and lunar limb modeling for Baily's beads and the diamond ring. Once the plan is set and the mount is ready, HelioMaker executes the sequence and prompts you at each filter moment, so you can watch the eclipse instead of operating software.

Solar Safety

Never look at the Sun through unfiltered optics. Except during the brief total phase of a total solar eclipse, use proper eye protection for direct viewing and secure a suitable solar filter over the front of every camera lens, telescope, binocular or finder pointed at the Sun. Unfiltered sunlight through optics can cause immediate, permanent eye injury and can damage cameras and other equipment.

  • Use a suitable solar filter secured over the front of the main imaging telescope throughout all partial phases.
  • If a separate guide scope is used, it requires its own solar filter.
  • Removing a filter during the brief total phase is an advanced procedure that depends on the selected eclipse mode and exact local circumstances. Follow the detailed eclipse guide and rehearse the procedure in advance.
  • HelioMaker's cues and automation assist the operator but do not replace safe solar imaging practice.

Prepare and Rehearse in Advance

An eclipse is a single event with strict timing constraints. Do not use eclipse day to learn the equipment, establish guiding for the first time, or test a new capture plan. Begin preparation weeks in advance.

  • Enter and verify the observer latitude, longitude, elevation, time zone, and computer clock.
  • Verify the predicted contact times against a trusted independent eclipse source.
  • With the physical eclipse rig and solar filter, determine a well exposed full solar disk exposure and camera gain. Enter them in Plan preferences with the matching solar filter ND rating. HelioMaker uses these inputs with Fred Espenak's eclipse exposure recommendations to derive the phase recipes.
  • Practice daytime polar alignment and solar guiding on ordinary sunny days.
  • If the plan will use automatic refocus, configure SharpCap Focus Assistant with the physical eclipse rig and complete a successful solar focus scan before enabling the option in HelioMaker. Manually confirm that SharpCap Refocus can repeat the result.
  • Build and approve the plan, then repeat both timing only and full equipment rehearsals.
  • Practice every filter action and become familiar with the audio and visual cues.
  • Verify storage capacity, power, camera performance, focus, and recovery procedures.

Do not tune sequence parameters manually in SharpCap after opening the preflight dialog. HelioMaker applies and verifies the saved plan's camera settings and applies them again when the run is armed. If testing requires a change, cancel preflight, update and save the plan, repeat any affected camera test, and rehearse the revision. Last minute SharpCap adjustments will be replaced by the plan.

Required Equipment

The exact configuration depends on whether guiding is enabled, but a typical automated eclipse rig includes:

  • A main imaging telescope and camera controlled by SharpCap Pro.
  • Suitable solar filtration for the telescope, camera, and selected eclipse procedure.
  • An equatorial mount capable of reliable tracking.
  • For full solar disk imaging, HelioMaker can guide from the shared primary imaging camera. Shared camera guiding pauses around C2 and C3 and throughout totality, then resumes and recenters the Sun after C3 when reliable limb detection returns.
  • A separate guide scope with its own camera and solar filter is preferred when guiding is required inside totality, or when the main camera images only part of the solar disk at high magnification. Shared camera guiding assumes that the main camera contains the entire solar disk with enough surrounding space for reliable detection.
  • The custom PHD2 version required by HelioMaker when guiding is enabled.
  • A Windows computer with an accurate clock, dependable power, and sufficiently fast storage.
  • A motorized focuser configured in SharpCap is needed when the plan uses automatic refocusing before totality. SharpCap must already have a successful saved solar focus scan using Contrast (Edge) Detection or Fourier Detail Detection; HelioMaker requests SharpCap Refocus rather than calculating its own focus curve.

Workflow Overview

  1. Select the eclipse and observer site. The planner calculates the local circumstances and displays the contact times and eclipse geometry.
  2. Define the capture plan in Plan preferences. Choose Safe or Diamond ring (Classical) imaging mode, choose Still mode or Live view, select the guiding configuration, and enter the measured full solar disk exposure, camera gain and filter ND rating. The eclipse mode affects filter timing and the derived imaging plan; Still versus Live affects how the plan is captured.
  3. Review the derived phase recipes. HelioMaker creates the starting exposures and brackets from the measured imaging base and Fred Espenak's eclipse exposure recommendations. Keeping the same gain across all phases is recommended. Use the phase editor for deliberate tuning.
  4. Review and approve the plan. Approval separates the rehearsed plan from later, unverified edits.
  5. Rehearse. Use a dry run that exercises only the timing, then a wet run with the intended camera or the eclipse simulator. Confirm that cues, filter actions, focusing, guiding, and file output behave as expected.
  6. Run preflight. Before arming, HelioMaker checks the plan, camera, focus, guiding, time and location, storage, power readiness, and other conditions that could compromise the run.
  7. Monitor the run console. During the eclipse, the console presents the active phase, clocks, capture progress, cues, guiding state, refocus state, and any missed or rejected work. A compact view retains the critical status and filter cues while using less screen space.

Determine exposure and gain before imaging starts. The current eclipse workflow locks the plan during a run, so exposure and gain cannot be changed after automated imaging begins. Measure them with the physical rig on an ordinary sunny day whenever possible, or no later than the initial partial phase before starting the run. A camera change made only in SharpCap does not update the planned phase recipes.

Simulation and Preflight Are Part of the Workflow

Simulation is not merely a demonstration mode. It is how the complete plan, timing, camera behavior, operator responses, and critical safety cues are validated before the real event. Likewise, the preflight dialog is a go or no go review rather than a formality. Resolve warnings before arming whenever possible; forcing a run transfers responsibility for every unresolved condition to the operator.
