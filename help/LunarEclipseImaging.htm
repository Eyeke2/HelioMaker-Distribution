Lunar Eclipse Imaging

HelioMaker includes a dedicated workflow for planning, rehearsing and running an automated capture of a lunar eclipse. Use it to find a lunar eclipse for your observing site, build adaptive imaging and guiding settings, rehearse the sequence, complete preflight and monitor capture through SharpCap and the custom PHD2 integration.

This chapter is an introduction to the feature. The separate Lunar Eclipse Imaging Guide provides detailed instructions organized by task. Read it, and rehearse the plan with your equipment, before eclipse night.

What the Lunar Workflow Does

The Lunar Eclipse Planner supports total, partial and penumbral events, with the phases and local visibility appropriate to the selected eclipse. Open it from Tools → Lunar Eclipse Planner or with Ctrl+Shift+L.

  • It finds a lunar eclipse for your observing site and shows its contacts and the Moon's visibility from that site.
  • Plan preferences set the rig reference, brightness assumptions, cadence and camera configuration used to derive the lunar plan.
  • The phase editor shows the adaptive exposure recipe and timing for each lunar phase.
  • A rehearsal runs the sequence on a simulated eclipse clock, and preflight verifies the current plan, clock and equipment before the sequence starts.
  • The run console shows phase timing, delivered images, guiding and recovery during the run.

Prepare and Rehearse in Advance

Build and rehearse the plan while there is time to change the setup.

  • Set the observer site in Dynamic Target Tracking before opening the planner, then confirm the coordinates, elevation and time zone in the planner header.
  • Check the Moon's altitude through the phases you want to record. A calculated horizon does not describe buildings, trees or your mount's clearance, so check those obstructions in advance.
  • Frame the Moon with enough margin for tracking error through the whole run, and confirm polar alignment, image scale and PHD2 calibration for the actual optics and guiding camera.
  • Establish a sharp focus baseline and repeatable exposure and gain settings on the Moon.
  • Test cables, power, storage and the capture folder with a sustained camera run.
  • Review the meridian crossing and mount limits. HelioMaker reports a crossing but does not command a meridian flip in this workflow.
  • Prove the plan in stages: save a draft, run the lunar camera benchmark for the selected setup, rehearse with timing only or rendered images, then rehearse with the real SharpCap camera and the chosen guiding arrangement. Review the saved images, achievable cadence and recovery prompts before saving the approved final plan.

Use preflight again on eclipse night. A successful rehearsal does not confirm the current camera connection, sky conditions or framing.

Required Equipment

HelioMaker coordinates lunar imaging through SharpCap and lunar tracking and guiding through its custom PHD2 integration. Prepare SharpCap Pro and the matching custom PHD2 installation before connecting the rig.

  • An imaging camera and configuration that pass the lunar System benchmark. A working preview alone does not verify long exposures, HDR transitions or saved-file delivery.
  • One of three camera arrangements, chosen in Plan preferences:
    • Shared imaging camera. Imaging and guide work use the same camera, and PHD2 uses the Virtual Planetary Camera. Verify the time left for guide work between brackets, especially during totality.
    • Separate guide camera. Choose Physical guiding camera and select that camera in PHD2. Set and test its own bright-Moon and dark-eclipse exposures, and confirm its field of view and calibration.
    • Guiding off. Set PHD2 guiding to Off in Plan preferences. The mount must preserve framing without guide corrections. Physical capture still requires lunar mount preparation through PHD2.
  • Enough storage and power for a multi-hour session.

Workflow Overview

The workflow to practice: set the site → plan → test → rehearse → approve → preflight → capture.

  1. Set your site. Open Dynamic Target Tracking and check the observer coordinates, elevation and time zone for the site where you will image.
  2. Find and select the eclipse. In the planner, set Window, click Find lunar eclipses, then inspect the contacts and Moon visibility. A past event is suitable for rehearsal.
  3. Set the imaging plan. In Plan preferences, enter your full-Moon reference and gain, choose the guiding camera arrangement, HDR capture mode, cadence and file settings, and review the brightness model and exposure ceiling.
  4. Review each phase. Inspect its adaptive exposure range, bracket, interval and guiding, then save your work with Save as draft.
  5. Test the camera and focus. Run the System benchmark test with the intended configuration, establish focus, and practice lunar tracking and guiding with the physical rig.
  6. Rehearse. Click Eclipse plan rehearsal.... Start with the internal simulator, then exercise the SharpCap camera and guiding at realistic timing, and inspect the output.
  7. Approve the plan. After reviewing and rehearsing the settings, use Save as final. A live run requires the saved final plan.
  8. Complete preflight on eclipse night. Click Start eclipse imaging. Verify the selected event, site and real clock; apply camera settings, prepare tracking and guiding, verify focus and exposure, and review every checklist item.
  9. Start and monitor. Start the run from preflight. Watch capture progress and alerts in the run console, and open the capture folder to check the saved images.

Start with an imaging reference measured for your rig, review the brightness assumptions and long exposures, and leave enough time for the camera and guiding to complete each set. A rehearsal should prove both the sequence and the resulting images.

Simulation and Preflight Are Part of the Workflow

A rehearsal runs the plan on a simulated eclipse clock. Choose its imaging source:

  • Eclipse simulator renders lunar images and writes them without a physical SharpCap camera.
  • SharpCap camera uses the simulated clock with the real imaging camera. Camera operations and image files are real.
  • None (timing only) exercises timing without writing science image files.

With shared camera guiding enabled, a rehearsal can also offer Simulated guiding frames, which feed rendered lunar frames to PHD2 to exercise lunar disk detection without the sky. A SharpCap camera rehearsal operates physical equipment, so check the selected source and guiding settings before starting. Rehearse with realistic timing when judging long exposures, camera overhead and guiding opportunities.

Preflight verifies the current plan, clock and equipment before the sequence starts. Read each item and complete its action or confirmation; some checks require your judgment rather than an automatic measurement. Review amber warnings even if the start control allows proceeding. A live start is blocked more than 12 hours before P1 and after the eclipse has ended.
